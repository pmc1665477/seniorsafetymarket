Bringing a Loved One Home From the Hospital or Rehab: A Room-by-Room Equipment Checklist

By the Senior Safety Market team · Updated 2026

The day a hospital or rehab facility says your loved one is ready to come home is a relief — and it's also a lot to handle at once. Discharge often happens faster than families expect, and the house that worked fine before the hospital stay may suddenly have gaps: no grab bar by the toilet, a bed that's hard to get in and out of, stairs that are now a real obstacle. This checklist walks through what to think about, room by room, so you can walk in the door prepared instead of scrambling.

Before They Arrive: Talk to the Discharge Team

Before your loved one leaves the facility, ask the discharge planner or nurse a few practical questions — they expect these and can usually answer on the spot:

  • What level of help will they need with walking, bathing, and getting in/out of bed?
  • Will they need a walker, wheelchair, or cane — and for how long?
  • Are there any specific home modifications they recommend (grab bars, ramps, etc.)?
  • Is a hospital bed or shower chair recommended for the first few weeks?
  • Who do we call if something doesn't fit or work once we're home?

These answers will tell you which of the items below actually apply to your situation — not every homecoming needs every item.

Bathroom

The bathroom is usually the highest-risk room in the house for falls, and often the room with the least advance warning needed.

  • Grab bars near the toilet and inside the shower or tub
  • Shower chair or bench so they can sit while bathing
  • Raised toilet seat to reduce strain on knees and hips
  • Non-slip mat or tape for the tub or shower floor

Bedroom

  • Bed rail or handle to help with getting in and out of bed safely
  • Electric hospital bed, if recommended — makes adjusting position much easier for both the patient and caregiver
  • A clear, unobstructed path from the bed to the bathroom — this is a good time to move rugs, cords, and clutter

Getting Around the House

  • Walker or rollator
  • Wheelchair or transport chair, if walking distances is limited right now
  • Threshold ramp for any step at the front or garage door
  • Stair lift, if their bedroom or bathroom is upstairs and stairs are a real concern

Health Monitoring & Daily Care

  • Blood pressure monitor, if their care team wants regular readings
  • Medical alert system for when they're home alone
  • Pill organizer to keep new medication schedules straight

A Budget-Friendly Tip

Most of these items are only needed for a matter of weeks or months for many people, and they're often barely used when purchased new.
